You stayed late in the seminary's rare-book room, and the archivist locked up without seeing you. Four cabinets each hold one letter of the door's combination. Open each one by reading scripture the way a careful interpreter does.

Each cabinet teaches one interpretive habit: knowing the genre, placing a text in history, weighing manuscripts, and comparing parallel passages. The clock starts when you open your first cabinet.

Why this activity

  • Each lock is a learning outcome. Students identify genre, place texts in history, apply a principle of textual criticism, and compare synoptic parallels. Each solved lock ends with a short "what you practiced" note.
  • Productive struggle, with a safety net. Wrong answers give specific feedback, and hints cost time rather than ending the game, so students keep reasoning instead of guessing.
  • More than clicking. Students drag passages onto shelves, reorder a timeline, sweep an ultraviolet lamp across a manuscript, and mark the exact words that differ between Gospels.
  • Ready for a course shell. It works as a module opener in a hybrid M.Div. or a D.Min. orientation. Scripture quotations are from the King James Version, which is in the public domain, so the game can be shared and adapted freely.
  • Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA): every drag has a keyboard equivalent, the lamp moves with arrow keys, and a text version of the hidden notes is always one click away.
